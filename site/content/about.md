# About

I met Umamusume the way most people do: as a joke. Cute horse girls, gacha, memes, a parody I was not meant to take seriously. I scrolled past it for a long time.

Then something broke. The fandom has a name for it, the breaking-illusion point: the moment you realize it was never a joke. Every character is built on a real horse, a real life, a real record, a real death, and the writers took all of it seriously, down to the grief. It is not a parody. It is not an idol story. There is a soul and a story behind every story.

After that you stop being a fan for the cuteness and the memes. You become a fan for the soul. That is where I have been ever since, and this site is what I do with it.

## What this is

UmaStudies is a personal project. Just me, writing deep, primary-text character studies of the cast of *Umamusume: Pretty Derby*, one character at a time.

Every study is built from the original Japanese: character stories, support card stories, the career scenario, home-screen lines, alongside the real-horse record behind each character. The Japanese is treated as the authority text; the English is my working translation. Each one is long because I would rather follow a character all the way down than write something short and safe.

This is not a content pipeline. It is the thing I make when I want to take these characters as seriously as the people who wrote them did. If a single study convinces one person that the depth is real, it has done its job.

## Spoilers

These are complete studies. They discuss each character's full story, endings included. If you would rather meet a character in-game first, read their study afterward.

## A note on pace

I'm a student, and this runs alongside university, a job, and other projects. I aim for roughly one character a week, but please don't hold me to a schedule. Each study takes real time to do properly, and I would rather post something with depth than something on time. This is a labor of love, made in the hours I can find.

## Corrections welcome

I'm not a native Japanese speaker, and I care about getting these right. If you spot a mistranslation, or you have a reading of a character you want to share, the feedback form at the bottom of each study comes straight to me. I read all of it.
